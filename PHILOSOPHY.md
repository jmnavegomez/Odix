# The Philosophy of Odix

This document serves as the project's manifesto.
> Ideas deserve permanence.
>
> Books deserve craftsmanship.
>
> Authors deserve better tools.
>
> Every Principia deserves an excellent Codex.

Books have never been mere collections of words.

For centuries they have been carefully composed, reviewed, typeset and finally printed. Every stage in this journey has carried its own craft, vocabulary and tradition.

Odix is built on the belief that modern technical publishing deserves the same care.

Instead of hiding the publishing process behind anonymous functions, Odix embraces the language of the book itself. Every component of the system represents a real stage in the historical creation of a manuscript.

A book enters the Scriptorium, becomes a Codex, receives its form through Typus, and is finally delivered by Impressio.

The software is therefore not only a compiler.

It is an editorial workshop.

## I. Why Odix Exists

For centuries, books have represented the highest form of technical communication.

Scientists, engineers and mathematicians have relied on books to preserve knowledge across generations.

Software has transformed how we write, but surprisingly little attention has been given to the craft of publishing technical books.

Odix exists because writing excellent technical books should be as reproducible as building excellent software.

## II. Books Deserve Build Systems

Books deserve reproducible build systems.

Technical books deserve tools that are reproducible, structured and built to last.

Writing software demands discipline.

Writing books deserves the same discipline.

Great books are remembered for their ideas.

Great editions ensure those ideas endure.

## III. The Journey of a Book

Every book begins as a Principia.

The Principia is analysed into a Tabula.

The Scriptorium compiles the Tabula into a Codex.

Typus gives the Codex its visual identity.

Impressio publishes the finished work.

## IV. The Language of the Project

The names used throughout the project are not decorative.

They describe the historical stages through which books have been created for centuries.

They preserve the vocabulary of centuries of bookmaking.

They form a common language shared by every contributor.

## V. Design Principles

### The source is sacred.

The author's Principia is the single source of truth.

It must remain independent from the build system.


### Deterministic builds. 

The same Principia and configuration should always produce the same publication.

Reproducibility is not a feature.

It is a requirement.

### Beautiful by default. 

Beauty is part of communication.

Typus exists so authors can focus on ideas instead of formatting.

### Human-readable first. 

Books are written by humans.

Their source should remain readable by humans.

### Open standards.

Knowledge should never depend on closed formats, proprietary workflows or inaccessible tools.

## VI. What Odix Will Never Be

Odix is not a word processor.

Odix is not a desktop publishing application.

Odix is not a visual editor.

Odix is not another Markdown flavour.

## VII. Looking Forward

We hope that one day authors will no longer say:

"I exported a PDF."

Instead they will simply say:

"I published my Principia."

> May every Principia become a worthy Codex.
